DRVN Media Eyes Strongest Quarter of the Year After AI-First Shift

After a slow year, the media marketing company is seeing more calls and new client signings across its Tampa, Des Moines and Lima footprint. The final size of the rebound remains uncertain.

By Tarbell Business Desk · Published Thu, Nov 5, 2026, 7:00 PM (America/Lima)

TAMPA–DES MOINES–LIMA — DRVN Media is on track for what it expects to be its strongest quarter of the year, as more calls and new client signings give the media marketing company a brighter fourth-quarter outlook after a very slow year. The signs of renewed activity are encouraging, but the scale of the recovery remains uncertain, with final results still to be determined.

The outlook spans a company with regional offices in Tampa, Des Moines and Lima. Management has described the year as tough, pointing to a pace of advancement in artificial intelligence that outstripped the team’s capabilities. That assessment places the company’s current optimism against a difficult backdrop: a business working to catch up with technology that was moving faster than its team could match.

In response, DRVN Media shifted to an AI-first approach. According to the company’s account, that change has advanced its capabilities and its sales teams. Rather than treating the technology’s rapid development only as a challenge, the company has made it central to its approach as it works to regain momentum.

The shift is now accompanied by more calls and new clients being signed during the fourth quarter. Those developments underpin expectations for the strongest quarter of the year. They point to a more active sales environment than the company experienced during its slow stretch, although increased activity alone does not establish the final financial outcome.

For management, the emerging picture is one of renewed possibility rather than a completed turnaround. The company’s account connects its improved capabilities and sales effort with its AI-first direction, but does not quantify how much of the latest business activity can be attributed to that change. The outlook is therefore more concrete about the direction of travel than the magnitude of the improvement.

There are still important limits to what can be concluded. The size of any growth and the final quarterly numbers remain uncertain. Expectations that Q4 could be the biggest quarter yet are a forecast, not a reported result, and the current account does not establish a record against every quarter in the company’s history.

For now, DRVN Media’s story across Tampa, Des Moines and Lima is a cautiously optimistic one: a tough year, an AI-first reset and a busier closing quarter. Whether that momentum delivers the year’s best performance will depend on how the rest of Q4 unfolds and where the final numbers land.
