Opinion: The Sunday Scaries Are a Manufactured Crisis—and We Keep Buying In

Every week we pretend Monday is a surprise. It is not. Here is how to stop the spiral.

By Riley Santos · Updated Sat, Oct 31, 2026, 5:45 PM (America/Lima)

Opinion

There is nothing natural about the dread that settles in around 4 p.m. on a Sunday. It is a ritual we rehearse: open laptop, glance at calendar, spiral.

We treat the Sunday Scaries like weather—inevitable, impersonal. They are neither. They are a story we tell ourselves about unfinished work and unearned rest.

A modest proposal: name one concrete thing you will do Monday morning, close the laptop, and go outside. The crisis shrinks when you stop rehearsing it.

Monday will arrive either way. You might as well meet it with a plan instead of a panic.
